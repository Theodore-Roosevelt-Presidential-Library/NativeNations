# Principles

Native Nations is a front door, not a guidebook. It helps travelers find the tourism and visitation resources that Native nations and their tourism alliances publish themselves, then sends them there.

## Nations speak for themselves
When a nation says who it is, that governs: its name, how it spells it, which people it belongs to, and whether it is separate from a people it has been grouped with. Federal, state, scholarly or older names are kept only as "also called" or "previously listed as." If a nation's own statement conflicts with any other source, the nation's statement wins, and the directory changes to match.

## Homelands and sacred lands
Where a nation has publicly described its homelands or named places as sacred, the directory may say so, in plain words, with a link to the nation's own publication. It does this only from the nation's own sources, never from outside accounts. It never draws homeland boundaries, never gives directions or coordinates to a sacred place, and never describes ceremonies. Sacred places appear only after the Library's Indigenous content reviewers, and ideally the nation, have approved that entry. Where a sacred place is also public land (a national forest, a state park), the directory can note how the nation asks people to treat it, again in the nation's words.

## What the directory shows
- Facts: the nation's official name and the name it uses for itself, state(s), headquarters town, distance to nearby public lands.
- Short tags a traveler can filter by ("cultural center", "guided tours", "powwow season: summer").
- Links: the nation's own tourism page, cultural center, events or powwow page, tour booking, visitor protocols, permits; its state tourism alliance; its Destination Native America page.
- At most one sentence of Library-written text per nation, approved before publishing.

## What it never shows
- Copied descriptions, photos, logos, event listings or itineraries.
- Locations, directions or ceremonial details for sacred places, and any burial sites.
- Homelands or sacred places described by anyone other than the nation itself.
- Homeland boundaries, territory maps or migration routes. (Link to native-land.ca instead.)
- Rankings or judgments of nations or their offerings.

## How links are kept honest
- Every outbound link carries UTM tags (`utm_source=trlibrary&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=nativenations`) so partners can see the traffic the Library sends.
- Links are checked on a schedule; broken links are flagged, not silently removed.
- Every record has `last_verified` and `review.status`.

## Working with partners
- Before a nation or alliance is listed beyond the federal basics, the Library contacts it to confirm the right landing pages.
- Partners can ask for any change or removal at any time, and the request is honored.
- Pilot: North Dakota's five nations, with the North Dakota Native Tourism Alliance.
